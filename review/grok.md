The common-drive rotating-wave claim that the SN and CWL zero-photon probabilities agree is internally consistent. The abstract’s stronger claim, that this replaces the published ratio \(e^2\) and replaces the prefactor by \(\pi^2\epsilon^4/4\), is not established. Major revision.

I did not open the Caltech record, the DOI, or the cited repository, and I used no local tools. Everything below is a rederivation from the supplied source. Unchecked against the journal are Eqs. 90, 100, 105, 107, 112, 114, and 122–138, including whether Eq. 126 really is the ordinary-area matrix.

Assumptions I had to grant: one common \(F(t)\) and laser frequency; \(\Delta=-\omega\); rectangular pulses with \(g_*=\omega/20\) and \(T=t_p\); replica-local couplings so one orthogonal rotation diagonalizes \(\omega^2\Pi+\Omega^2(I-\Pi)\) into a collective oscillator \((\omega,g_\omega)\) and \(N-1\) relative oscillators \((\Omega,g_\Omega)\); \(K_{j,N}=\mathrm{per}(a_j I+(u_j-a_j)\Pi)\); probabilities \(|K_{j,N}|^{2/N}\) normalized only over \(j=0,1\); \(N\to\infty\) before \(h\to 0\).

**Permanent.** With \(z=(u-a)/a\) and \(c=z/N\), \(\mathrm{per}(I+cJ)=\sum_k(N)_k(z/N)^k\). Choosing the \(k\) rows that take the rank-one entry forces those columns to be a permutation, giving \(k!\binom{N}{k}\). The integral identity is exact for every complex \(z\), because \((1+zt/N)^N\) is a polynomial and \(\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^k\,dt=k!\). Thus \(K/a^N\to 1/(1-z)\) whenever \(\mathrm{Re}(1-z)>0\). At a perfect ordinary return, \(a_{(0,\omega)}=0\), so the endpoint factor \(\sqrt{\omega/\Omega}\) keeps \(u_0=0\) and \(z=-1\), and the limit is \(1/2\neq 0\). Then \(|1/2|^{2/N}\to 1\), so the rooted weight tends to \(|a_{(0,\Omega)}|^2\), not to a Stirling-suppressed collective weight. For \(|z+1|\le 0.1\), \(|1+zt/N|\le 1-(1-\eta)t/N\) on \([0,N]\) yields the integrable dominant \(e^{-(2-\eta)t}\). The tail bound \(e^{-N}(1+\eta)^N e^{N\eta/(1+\eta)}N!/N^N\) is correct, and Stirling gives rate \(e^{-cN}\) with \(c=2-\ln 1.1-0.1/1.1\approx 1.81\). For \(|z|\le 1/4\) the same limit follows by geometric domination. Both limits are uniform on those disks. The bright and dark weights therefore tend to \(|a_{(j,\Omega)}|^2\), which already sum to 1. Inside this model the ratio is 1.

That is a common-input statement: \(F\) and the laser are fixed, and area is the ordinary area, so the collective amplitude is the one that vanishes. The factor \(e^2\) is a different experiment. If the area is instead the relative-mode area, then \(a_0=0\), \(K=N!(u/N)^N\), and \((N!)^{1/N}/N\to 1/e\). Applying Eq. 122 to the ordinary-area matrix would be an algebraic mistake. Choosing the relative-mode area on purpose would be a protocol change. The source does not supply the journal algebra that would decide which of those Eq. 126 is. The headline treats them as one correction.

**Perturbations.** With \(r=(1-2h)^{1/4}=1-h/2+O(h^2)\) and no detuning, the two pulse angles sum to \(r(\pi+\delta)\) and \(a_{(0,\Omega)}=i(\delta-\pi h/2)+O(h^2)\). The hold does not feed \(a_0\) at order \(h\). On the ideal trajectory the Duhamel integrand is \(i\sin\phi\cos\phi\) on the first ramp and \(-i\sin\beta\cos\beta\) on the second; those integrals cancel, and during the hold the remaining \(\pi/2\) pulse maps \(|m\rangle\) entirely to the optical state, so the integrand is zero. The hold phase of order \(10\pi h\) appears in \(a_1\), not in the dark probability. An \(O(h^2)\) amplitude error changes that probability only at \(O(h^3)=O(\epsilon^6)\) if \(h=\epsilon^2\). The cancellation survives.

The numerical bounds are weaker than the prose. \(\int\|\delta H\|\,dt\le 100h\) is true for \(h\le 0.01\), but \(\sum_{n\ge 2}\varepsilon^n/n!\le\varepsilon^2/2\) is false near \(\varepsilon=105h\sim 1\). At \(h=0.01\), \(10^4 h^2=1\) is larger than the leading amplitude \(\pi h/2\), so it does not control \(\pi^2 h^2/4\) on the whole interval used to advertise the remainder. It is adequate at the later disk \(h\le 10^{-6}\): the leading probability is \(\pi^2 h^2/4\approx 2.47\times 10^{-12}\), and \(10^4 h^2\) moves it by about one percent.

**Coefficient.** Under the printed dictionary, \(|a_0|^2=\pi^2 h^2/4\). The manuscript also quotes the journal coefficients \(\pi^2/16\) and \(\pi^2/(16e^2)\) and sets \(h=\epsilon^2\). Those cannot all be right: the new prefactor is four times the quoted SN prefactor, not that prefactor with \(e^2\) removed. Literal inversion of the kinetic factor \(1-h\) does not repair Eq. 107 either: \((1+h/2)/(1-h)=1+3h/2+O(h^2)\). The square-root rescaling does: \((1+h/2)(1-h)^{-1/2}=1+h+O(h^2)\).

The counter-rotating lower bound checks. At zero gravity the hold is \(e^{-i10\pi}=1\), so the pulses concatenate to \(t=\pi/g_*\). The frequencies \(\sqrt{\omega(\omega\pm 2g_*)}\) and \(u_\pm=\cos(\omega_\pm t)-i(\omega\pm g_*)\sin(\omega_\pm t)/\omega_\pm\) are correct, and \(A_1/A_0=1/u^*\), \(|A_0|^2=1/|u|\) give the printed \(B\). Hand evaluation yields \(B\approx 1.567\times 10^{-5}\). The ordinary background is about \(10^7\) times the rotating-wave signal at \(h=10^{-6}\), so the physical ratio is not determined.
