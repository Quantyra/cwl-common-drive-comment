# Referee Report — Comment on "Testing quantum gravity using pulsed optomechanical systems" (v1.0.0)

**Verdict: Major revision.** The central mechanism identified is, as far as I can verify, correct and worth publishing: the $e^{-2}$ in Ref. [1]'s Eq. (126) is a permanent-of-a-rank-one-perturbation artifact that appears only when the *relative*-sector dark amplitude is set exactly to zero. But the headline claim as stated ("the ratio is one") is weaker and more protocol-dependent than the Comment admits, and the manuscript misses a model-dependent term that enters one power of $h$ *below* the quantity it compares.

**What I checked.** I did not open the Caltech record or the DOI; all statements about Ref. [1] rest on the orientation facts supplied and are flagged as such. I verified independently: the sector decomposition $\omega^2\Pi+\Omega^2(I-\Pi)$ from $\Omega^2 I-\omega_{\rm SN}^2 J/N$; $r=\sqrt{\omega/\Omega}=(1-2h)^{1/4}$ given fixed optical frequency pull; the two-pulse amplitudes $a_{0,\omega}=i\sin\delta$, $a_{1,\omega}=-\cos\delta$ and the area deficit $-\pi h/2$; the permanent identity $\mathrm{perm}(aI+cJ)=a^N\sum_k (N)_k(z/N)^k=a^N\!\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(1+zt/N)^N dt$, the $z=-1$ limit $1/2$ and the tail $e^{-N}N!/N^N$; the $u_\pm$ Bogoliubov coefficients as $\tfrac12(\nu/\omega+\omega/\nu)=(\omega\pm g_*)/\omega_\pm$; and $B$, which I reproduce as $1.5669\times10^{-5}$ (hand arithmetic, ~4 digits), consistent with the quoted $1.56738\times10^{-5}$. I confirm the detuning cancellation: the insertion is $\propto\int\sin 2\phi\,dt$, which gives $+1/g_*$ on pulse 1, $-1/g_*$ on pulse 2, zero on the hold. I did not verify Eqs. (90), (105), (107), (110), (112), (114), (122), (126), (138).

## Findings

**1. [Major] The headline equality is one branch of a discontinuous limit; the Comment states the weaker version of its own result.** With $u_0\neq0$ fixed, $|K_{0,N}|^{2/N}\to|a_0|^2$ for $a_0\neq0$ but equals $|u_0|^2(N!/N^N)^{2/N}\to e^{-2}|u_0|^2$ at $a_0=0$. The limit function is *discontinuous* at $a_0=0$: the crossover sits at $|a_0|\sim|u_0|/N$, i.e. a pulse-area window $|\delta-\pi h/2|\lesssim \pi h/(2N)$, which vanishes as $N\to\infty$. This is the decisive argument and it is absent. It also shows the framing is wrong: Ref. [1]'s $e^2$ is not merely a mismatched-input comparison but an unstable limit, unattainable at any finite area tolerance. Conversely, at $\Delta=-\Omega$ with $g_\Omega$ calibration the Comment's own machinery gives $P_{\rm SN}=0$ and $P_{\rm CWL}=e^{-2}\pi^2h^2/4$ — ratio $0$, not $1$. So "one" is a statement about the bare-reference protocol, not a correction of Ref. [1] *simpliciter*. **The Comment does both things the brief distinguishes**: it legitimately corrects a common-input comparison *and* substitutes a protocol, while presenting the outcome as protocol-free. Rewrite the abstract and the "Scope" section to say: the SN/CWL ratio is protocol-dependent, equals one at bare-reference calibration, and the $e^{-2}$ branch requires relative-mode area control to $O(h/N)$.

**2. [Major] Leading-order term missed: the counter-rotating sector's *model-dependent* part is $O(h)$.** $B$ is computed at zero gravity and presented as a static background. But $\omega_\pm$ and the phases $\pi\sqrt{440},\pi\sqrt{360}$ track the mechanical frequency, which is $\Omega$ in the relative sector and $\omega$ collectively. Since $\mathrm{Im}(1/u_+^*-1/u_-^*)=0.00789$ arises from near-cancellation of $0.0749$ and $0.0827$, and $\cos\phi_\pm\approx-1$, shifting $\phi_\pm$ by $\phi_\pm h$ changes that difference by $\approx-(65.9-59.6)h=-6.3h$. At $h=10^{-6}$ this is $\delta B\approx 2\times(6.3\times10^{-6}/0.00789)\times1.57\times10^{-5}\approx2.5\times10^{-8}$ — about $10^4$ times the $2.47\times10^{-12}$ RWA signal, and *not* removable by background subtraction because it carries the same $h$-dependence as the hypothesis under test. Order-of-magnitude only; please compute it properly. This both strengthens the Comment's caveat and undercuts the operational meaning of any $O(h^2)$ comparison, Ref. [1]'s included.

**3. [Major] Normalization collision in $\epsilon$.** "Put $h=\epsilon^2$" is incompatible with $h=\omega_{\rm SN}^2/(2\Omega^2)$ if $\epsilon^2=\omega_{\rm SN}^2/\omega_m^2$. With $h=\epsilon^2/2$, $\pi^2h^2/4=\pi^2\epsilon^4/16$, matching Ref. [1]'s SN coefficient; as written the abstract's $\pi^2\epsilon^4/4$ disagrees with Eq. (138)'s SN value by $4\times$, which contradicts the claim that only the CWL side changes. Define $\epsilon$ once, in Ref. [1]'s convention, and state the relation to $h$.

**4. [Moderate] The claimed Eq. (107)/(112) discrepancy is retracted by its own paragraph.** If the collective metric contributes $(1-h)^{-1/2}\simeq1+h/2$, then $(1+h/2)^2=1+h$ and there is no error. Either show the arithmetic that leaves a residue or demote this from the abstract to "a normalization step not made explicit."

**5. [Moderate] The signal *is* the calibration mismatch.** The entire $\pi h/2$ comes from $r$, i.e. from $x_{\rm zpf}\propto\nu^{-1/2}$ at fixed $\partial\omega_c/\partial x$, mass and intracavity photon number — state this assumption explicitly. Any experimenter who calibrates the swap empirically (maximize photon return) nulls $a_0$ and the $O(h^2)$ dark count vanishes in both models. Note also that $|d_i|\le h/100$ at $h=10^{-6}$ demands area control to $10^{-8}$.

**6. [Moderate] Coherent-drive/Fock tension in the parity argument.** $F(t)$ is called "the classical intracavity drive," which implies $\langle a\rangle\neq0$ and a mean radiation-pressure force — which breaks "centered state + bilinear drive $\Rightarrow\langle q\rangle=0$." The conclusion survives (Ehrenfest gives the mean at $\omega$, fluctuations at $\Omega$), but say so: linearize about a strong pump, let $a$ be the signal mode prepared in $|1\rangle$, absorb the mean displacement into the origin, and check that this redefinition does not feed $a_0$ at $O(h)$.

**7. [Minor] Replica preparation/readout.** State that Ref. [1]'s one-derivative-per-replica kernel means one photon *per replica*, that the RWA single-excitation result supplies only the matrix elements $a_j,u_j$, and that mixed-replica endpoints are excluded by Eqs. (124)–(126). The weights' summing to one is then a property of the prescription, not an independent consistency check — present it as the former.

**8. [Minor] Drop the unearned constants** ($100h$, $210h^2$, $10^4h^2$ — vacuous at $h=10^{-2}$) in favour of derived $O(h^2)$ statements; fix the units of $F$ ($F q$ is not an energy as written); and, given the AI-assistance note, have finding 3 and the $B$ value human-checked before submission.
