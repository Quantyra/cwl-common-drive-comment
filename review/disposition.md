## Disposition of the findings

1. **Keep the theorem and narrow the headline.** For one fixed physical force, bare laser detuning, same-phase pulses, the given per-model centered preparation and the paper’s two exclusive endpoints, the two CWL probabilities agree with centered SN in the passive model. The result holds after taking the replica limit at fixed positive $h$ in the specified calibration box. Journal Eq. (103) selects the shifted rather than bare sideband. The manuscript now identifies both settings and distinguishes the change of protocol from the internal Eq. (107)/(112) discrepancy. A universal replacement of the published ratio would exceed the calculation.
2. **Explain the coefficient.** The journal defines $\epsilon^2=\omega_{\mathrm{SN}}^2/(2\Omega^2)$ before Eq. (87), so $h=\epsilon^2$. A change of notation cannot account for the factor of four. From Eqs. (134) and (137) with $n=0$, $2g t_p=\pi(1-2h)^{1/4}=\pi-\pi h/2+O(h^2)$. The first-order detuning contributions cancel between equal pulses; the hold contributes zero to the dark amplitude at this order. Squaring gives $\pi^2 h^2/4$ versus the $\pi^2 h^2/16$ printed in Eq. (138). See the algebra and error scope in the candidate.
3. **Keep the two printed equations distinct.** With the journal’s own means and $A=1,B=0$, (107) and the average of (112) predict derivatives differing by $-igh/2$. The equation being inverted is (105), with a bare $gA$ on its right-hand side. The disputed $g(1+h/2)A$ is obtained separately from (112). The endpoint normalization cannot reconcile two different instantaneous derivatives in the same coordinates. The candidate writes (105) explicitly.
4. **Strengthen the experimental limitation.** The ordinary lower bound in the paper survives. At fixed $\omega/g_*=20$, the full centered SN quadratic theory has a nonzero first derivative in $h$ of its *inclusive* probability for zero optical photons. The block derivative gives $-0.0126237111882$; at $h=10^{-6}$ the probability changes by $-1.26174558917\times10^{-8}$ from its zero-gravity value, compared with $2.4674\times10^{-12}$ for the nominal passive-model dark probability. A common constant subtraction leaves the linear change. This verifies the substance of Claude’s concern and supersedes its unsigned hand estimate. An inclusive CWL probability or SN/CWL difference does not follow from this calculation. [Derivation and reproducibility](centered-sn-slope.md)
5. **Preserve the proved root domain.** Grok checked both complex disks and the finite permanent correctly. Its suggestion that $\operatorname{Re}(1-z)>0$ suffices everywhere is false. For $z=-10$ and even $N$ the integrand is nonnegative, and the tail from $N/10$ equals $e^{-N/10}(10/N)^N N!$, which grows exponentially. Claude’s proposed discontinuity and $O(h/N)$ calibration requirement also fail. Put $b=u-a$, $v=a/b$. The same finite permanent is $N!(b/N)^N\sum_{j=0}^N(Nv)^j/j!$. For $|v|\le1/32$ the omitted exponential tail is at most $(N|v|)^ {N+1}/((N+1)!(1-|v|))$, exponentially small relative to $|e^{Nv}|$. Thus the rooted weight near $a=0$ is $e^{-2}|b|^2e^{2\operatorname{Re}v}$, continuous for fixed $u\ne0$. Neither extrapolation was adopted.
6. **Clarify the accuracy and physical controls.** A twice-iterated Duhamel formula retains the exact unitary as the last factor. Its norm bounds the ordered second integral by one half the square of the integrated perturbation, without truncating an exponential Taylor series. The calibration theorem only uses $h\le10^{-6}$. The draft spells out the distinction between the classical pump and optical fluctuations, absence of residual one-mode drives, per-replica Fock endpoints, and $10^{-8}$ radians of allowed error per pulse at $h=10^{-6}$. Empirical retuning defines another protocol; it does not generically null both gravity predictions.
